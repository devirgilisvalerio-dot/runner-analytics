How these predictions work ▸

The estimate uses your last 15 runs over 4 km. Recent runs count more than older ones.

Each run is adjusted to the race distance: the pace changes by about 3.5% for every doubling (or halving) of distance. So if your past runs are shorter than the target, the predicted pace is slower; if they're longer, it's faster; same distance, the pace stays unchanged.

If you haven't run for more than 7 days, the estimate slows by 0.5% for each additional day off, up to a maximum of +20%.

If the race is longer than your longest run in the last 365 days, the extra distance is penalized: about +7% slower for every time the target distance exceeds your longest run (for example, a marathon (42 km) is twice a 21 km longest run, so it's penalized ~7%).

Acute:Chronic Workload Ratio (ACWR) sweet spot 0.8–1.3

ACWR compares your last 7 days of load (km) against your 28-day average. 0.8–1.3 = safe training zone. > 1.3 = overreaching, elevated injury risk. > 1.5 = high injury risk. < 0.8 = undertraining / deload week.

Fitness / Fatigue / Form

CTL (Fitness) = 42-day rolling average of training load (TRIMP). Rises slowly with consistent training.
ATL (Fatigue) = 7-day rolling average of TRIMP. Spikes after hard weeks, drops during rest.
TSB (Form) = CTL − ATL. Your readiness to perform.

TSB +10 to +25 = peak form, ready to race.  TSB 0 to +10 = fresh, good for quality sessions.  TSB −10 to −30 = building fitness, manageable fatigue.  TSB < −30 = high fatigue, rest needed.

Polarized Training Check

Elite endurance athletes follow the 80/20 rule: ~80% of training at low intensity (Z2), ~20% at high intensity (Z4–Z5), and minimal time in the "grey zone" (Z3).

Easy (Z1–Z2) = below 80% max HR. Builds aerobic base, promotes recovery.
Grey zone (Z3) = 80–88% max HR. Hard to recover from, easy to build speed. Avoid spending more than 5–10% here.
Hard (Z4–Z5) = above 88% max HR. Intervals, threshold work. This is where speed improves.

Personal Records

Your fastest verified times at key distances (PRs). If no new PRs appear for weeks, consider changing your training stimulus (more intervals, more volume, or better recovery).

Pace Distribution

How much time you spend at each pace. Bars to the left mean fast running; bars to the right mean easy or recovery effort. A well-trained runner typically shows a broad aerobic base (5:00–6:00) with a sharp spike at race pace.

Running Efficiency (Power vs Pace)

Each dot is one run. X-axis = average power output (watts); Y-axis = average pace. Dots moving toward the bottom-left over time mean you're running faster with less effort — the key sign of improving fitness.
